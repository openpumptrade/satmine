            SATMINEbitcoin

            Fees mine Bitcoin.

            The coin's creator rewards buy real SHA-256 hashpower on NiceHash and the Bitcoin lands on a public address. Every hop is a receipt: Solana transactions, a marketplace order you can watch drain, and coinbase payouts on the chain.

            Hashpower bought0accepted speed

            01Claim

            The engine wallet claims the coin's creator rewards from pump.fun. SOL, on chain.

            02Convert

            Everything above a gas reserve is swapped on Solana through Jupiter into USDT, the unit NiceHash's SHA-256 market trades in. One transaction, the signature is the receipt.

            03Deposit

            The USDT goes over the Solana network to the marketplace deposit address shown below. It is never held: swapped and deposited in the same cycle.

            04Order

            One standing SHA256AsicBoost order on NiceHash, pointed at our pool and priced just above market so rigs pick it up at once. The budget drains by the second as rigs deliver; every later cycle refills the same order. Fees in, hashrate out, no subscription.

            05Mine

            The pool pays the public address straight from the block's coinbase when it finds one. Hashrate and payouts are read from the pool and from mempool.space.

            06Repeat

            Every 10 minutes. Each claim becomes a new rental; nothing waits for a minimum order.

            SATMINEhow it works

            How it works

            From a random seed to SOL in your wallet.

            01Ticket

            Your tab asks the server for a challenge bound to your address: a random 32-byte seed and a difficulty in bits. Tickets last two minutes.

            02Search

            Web Workers on your CPU and a WebGPU compute shader on your GPU hash SHA-256(seed ‖ nonce), looking for a digest that starts with difficulty zero bits.

            03Proof

            A hit is sent as its nonce. The server checks it with a single hash. Weak, reused, expired or borrowed proofs are rejected.

            04Credit

            A proof at difficulty d is credited 2d hashes, the number of tries it takes on average.

            05Difficulty

            Each address gets its own difficulty, tuned for about one proof every few seconds, so a phone and a GPU rig are measured the same way.

            06Round

            At round close the pot is split by share of verified work. Balances over the threshold are paid out and logged in the ledger.
